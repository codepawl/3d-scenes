# Sources

- Cockroach model: "Asian Cockroach" by peternak3d, https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/5b837eb6d68e45eeac4cf939b98a3574,
  CC BY 4.0, fetched through the Objaverse mirror (allenai/objaverse). Materials made matte in code.
- three.js r186 (MIT) and addons, vendored from `demos/ascii-depths/web/vendor`.
- JetBrains Mono Bold (SIL OFL 1.1).
- Facts: Full, R. J. & Tu, M. S. (1991) Mechanics of a rapid running insect: two-, four- and six-legged locomotion.
  J Exp Biol 156:215.
- Leg kinematics: Watson, J. T. & Ritzmann, R. E. (1998) Leg kinematics and muscle activity during treadmill
  running in the cockroach, Blaberus discoidalis: I. Slow running. J Comp Physiol A 182:11-22. Read from
  https://case.edu/artsci/biol/ritzmann/ (PDF). Used: the hip joint moves little; coxa-femur and femur-tibia extend in
  stance and flex in swing; femur-tibia excursion 26 deg middle leg, 52 deg hind leg; hind tibia thrusts rearward.
- Body motion while running: Weihmann, T., Brun, P. G. & Pycroft, E. (2017) Speed dependent phase shifts and gait
  changes in cockroaches running on substrates of different slipperiness. Front Zool 14:54 (PMC5719566). N. cinerea,
  peak to peak: lateral 1.55 mm, yaw 7-8 deg, pitch 5-7.5 deg, on a 27 mm body. Used as the size of sway, yaw and bob.
- Speeds and bipedal running: Full & Tu 1991 (above), as quoted by citing pages: 1.0-1.5 m/s (about 50 body lengths
  per second) on four then two legs, hind legs only at top speed. The paper itself was behind a bot check; body
  angle in the bipedal run is not in any source read, so the 15 deg here is a judgement.
- Wingbeat: 25-30 Hz for P. americana, reported by secondary sources; not read at the primary. The film slows it to
  6 Hz. No source on takeoff kinematics was found; the crouch, wing-opening order and leg tuck are judgement.
- Beetle model: "Blaps mortisaga", https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/d7d0e0204d4a4a5cb428f021d9429487, CC0, via the
  Objaverse mirror; decimated to 24k faces by tools/prepare_beetle.py. A darkling beetle (Tenebrionidae), the family of
  the flour beetle (Tribolium) that beat the roaches in the MythBusters radiation test; shown as its stand-in.
- Story facts: see topic.md "Facts and their sources" (Jayaram & Full 2016 PNAS; Scientific American 2007-03-15;
  MythBusters episode 97, 2008-01-30).
